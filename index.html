● Dealer Assess
Business Analyst Case Study

Which dealers need
management attention?

Dealer Assess reads monthly dealer data, uses clustering to discover hidden performance patterns, and gives regional managers a ranked list: who needs attention, why, and what to do first.

How the solution works

1

Ingest

24 months × 150 dealers of sales, inventory, payments, service, complaints. Upload your own file anytime.
2

Profile

Each dealer becomes one row: last-6-month averages plus sales momentum vs the previous 6 months.
3

Cluster

K-means groups dealers by similar behaviour, so patterns surface that no single metric shows.
4

Prioritise

A risk score weighted by market potential ranks dealers, explains drivers and suggests actions.
Priority score in short

A 0–100 number showing how urgently a dealer needs attention.

1 · Compare

Each metric is compared with the network average.

2 · Count only the bad

Only deviations in the wrong direction add points: low sales, ageing stock, late payments, weak service, more complaints, slowing momentum.

3 · Weight and scale

Points are multiplied by market potential (High ×1.25, Medium ×1, Low ×0.85) and scaled so the worst dealer scores 100. 55+ or a Critical group means “act now”.

Assumptions

Data

  • The manufacturer’s dealer network is in India, split into five regions: North, South, East, West and Central.
  • Synthetic dataset: 150 dealers, 5 regions, 24 months (Jan-2024 onward), 3,600 rows.
  • Rows are in chronological order per dealer; the latest 6 months represent “current” health.
  • Each dealer has a fixed synthetic latitude/longitude, used for the map. Uploaded files need the same columns as the sample (map columns optional).

Modelling & business logic

  • Features: sales vs target, growth, inventory ageing, payment delay, service, complaints, sales momentum.
  • Higher is good for sales, growth, service, momentum; lower is good for the other three.
  • Equal feature weights after z-score standardisation; k = 4 clusters (named by average health).
  • Priority score = sum of adverse deviations × potential factor (High 1.25, Medium 1.0, Low 0.85): a weak dealer in a rich market costs more.
  • “Emerging concern” = currently in a healthier cluster but sales momentum has dropped sharply.
  • Model is trained on a random 80% of dealers (seeded) and tested on the held-out 20%.
  • Suggested actions are rule-based playbooks and should be validated by regional managers.

Tools & deployment

Tools used

  • ChatGPT: problem breakdown and synthetic dataset prompt
  • Tonic Fabricate: generation of synthetic dataset
  • Claude: problem breakdown, prototype and code generation
  • HTML / CSS / vanilla JavaScript: the whole app, no backend
  • Chart.js: visuals · IBM Plex Sans: typography
  • K-means++ in JS: runs in the browser, so uploaded data never leaves the user’s device
  • GitHub + GitHub Pages: version control and hosting · BrownColour/dealer-assess

Proposed production path

  • Data: connect DMS / ERP / CRM monthly feeds; automate refresh.
  • Model: move clustering to Python (scikit-learn), add monitoring and managers’ feedback loop.
  • Geography: extend beyond India to more countries: country-specific maps, regions, currencies and calendars, with the same model re-trained per market.
  • Product: role-based views, alerts to regional managers, action tracking.

ⓘ How the priority score is calculated

Each metric is compared with the network average. Only deviations in the wrong direction add points (low sales, ageing stock, late payments, weak service, more complaints, slowing momentum). The total is multiplied by market potential (High ×1.25, Medium ×1, Low ×0.85) and scaled so the worst dealer scores 100. A score of 55+ or a Critical group means “need action now”.
